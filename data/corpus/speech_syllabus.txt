Speech Understanding Course Syllabus and Technical Vocabulary

Unit 1: Introduction to Speech Processing
Speech is a complex acoustic signal produced by the human vocal tract.
Automatic speech recognition ASR converts spoken language to text.
The acoustic model maps audio features to phoneme probabilities.
The language model assigns probabilities to word sequences.
The pronunciation dictionary maps words to phoneme sequences.

Unit 2: Signal Processing Fundamentals
The short-time Fourier transform STFT computes frequency content over time.
The spectrogram is a visual representation of frequency over time.
Mel filterbanks apply perceptually motivated frequency scaling.
Mel frequency cepstral coefficients MFCC are widely used acoustic features.
The cepstrum is the inverse Fourier transform of the log spectrum.
Cepstral mean normalization removes channel effects from features.
Pre-emphasis filtering boosts high-frequency components of speech.
Frame blocking segments the speech signal into overlapping frames.
Windowing functions like Hamming prevent spectral leakage.
Zero crossing rate ZCR measures signal polarity changes per frame.
Energy contour tracks the root mean square RMS amplitude over time.

Unit 3: Acoustic Modeling
Hidden Markov Models HMM model temporal variability in speech.
The Viterbi algorithm finds the most probable state sequence in an HMM.
The forward-backward algorithm computes state occupancy probabilities.
Gaussian mixture models GMM model the emission probability of HMM states.
Deep neural networks DNN replaced GMM for acoustic modeling in hybrid systems.
Context-dependent triphone models capture coarticulation effects.
Baum-Welch algorithm performs expectation-maximization EM for HMM training.
The phoneme is the smallest unit of sound in a language.
Allophones are contextual variants of phonemes.
Coarticulation describes how adjacent phonemes influence each other.

Unit 4: Language Modeling
N-gram language models estimate word probability from preceding words.
A bigram model conditions on the previous one word.
A trigram model conditions on the previous two words.
Perplexity measures language model quality on held-out data.
Kneser-Ney smoothing handles the zero-probability problem in n-gram models.
Neural language models use recurrent networks or transformers.
Backoff and interpolation are common smoothing strategies.
Vocabulary coverage and out-of-vocabulary OOV rate affect ASR accuracy.
Word error rate WER measures the edit distance between reference and hypothesis.
Beam search finds approximate best sequences by keeping top-k partial hypotheses.

Unit 5: End-to-End ASR
Connectionist temporal classification CTC enables framewise output alignment.
Attention-based encoder-decoder models jointly optimize acoustic and linguistic modeling.
The transformer architecture uses multi-head self-attention.
Wav2Vec 2.0 uses contrastive learning on raw waveforms.
Whisper is a large-scale weakly supervised ASR model.
The Whisper model uses a log-mel spectrogram as input.
The encoder processes acoustic features and the decoder generates tokens.
Constrained beam search restricts the output vocabulary during decoding.
Logit biasing adds a scalar reward to preferred token logits during generation.
The forced decoder ids mechanism forces language and task tokens in Whisper.

Unit 6: Speaker Recognition and Verification
Speaker verification determines whether two speech samples are from the same speaker.
Speaker identification classifies an utterance among a set of known speakers.
D-vectors are speaker embeddings extracted from deep neural networks.
X-vectors are speaker embeddings based on TDNN temporal delay neural networks.
ECAPA-TDNN is a state-of-the-art speaker embedding architecture.
Cosine similarity compares speaker embeddings.
The equal error rate EER is the point where false acceptance equals false rejection.

Unit 7: Text-to-Speech Synthesis
Text-to-speech TTS converts written text to spoken audio.
Concatenative synthesis splices pre-recorded speech segments.
Statistical parametric synthesis uses vocoders and acoustic models.
VITS variational inference with adversarial learning is an end-to-end TTS model.
Tacotron 2 predicts mel spectrograms from text using attention.
WaveNet is a deep generative model of raw audio waveforms.
HiFi-GAN is a generative adversarial network GAN vocoder for high-fidelity synthesis.
The fundamental frequency F0 determines the perceived pitch of speech.
Prosody refers to the rhythm stress and intonation of speech.
Duration modeling controls the timing of phonemes in synthesized speech.
Dynamic time warping DTW aligns two temporal sequences by warping the time axis.
MOS mean opinion score evaluates subjective speech quality.
Mel cepstral distortion MCD measures spectral distance between reference and synthesized speech.

Unit 8: Language Identification
Language identification LID classifies the language spoken in an utterance.
Code-switching occurs when two languages are mixed in a single utterance.
Hinglish is the code-mixed form of Hindi and English common in India.
Frame-level LID classifies language at each short time frame.
Multi-head classification produces multiple per-frame language decisions.
The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall.
Language boundary detection identifies switch points between languages.
Timestamp precision measures accuracy of detected switch points in milliseconds.

Unit 9: Robustness and Security
Adversarial perturbations are small input modifications that fool classifiers.
The fast gradient sign method FGSM generates adversarial examples using gradient sign.
Signal-to-noise ratio SNR measures the ratio of signal power to noise power.
Anti-spoofing countermeasure CM systems distinguish live speech from synthesized audio.
Linear frequency cepstral coefficients LFCC are used in anti-spoofing systems.
Constant-Q cepstral coefficients CQCC provide constant-Q frequency resolution.
The ASVspoof challenge evaluates anti-spoofing systems.
Bona fide samples are genuine human recordings.
Spoof samples are generated by voice conversion or TTS systems.
Replay attacks present a recording of a genuine speaker to fool a verification system.

Unit 10: Low-Resource and Multilingual Speech
Transfer learning adapts models trained on high-resource languages to low-resource ones.
Zero-shot cross-lingual transfer applies a model to languages unseen during training.
Voice cloning synthesizes speech in the voice of a reference speaker.
Speaker adaptation fine-tunes a general TTS model with few minutes of target speaker data.
Meta-MMS massively multilingual speech models cover thousands of languages.
Gondi is a Dravidian language spoken in central India.
Santhali Maithili and Gondi are low-resource languages LRL of interest.
IPA International Phonetic Alphabet provides a standardized phoneme notation.
Grapheme-to-phoneme G2P conversion maps written words to their pronunciation.
Romanization converts a script to Latin characters for common processing.
Parallel corpus is a set of sentences in two or more languages aligned by meaning.
Stochastic gradient descent SGD optimizes neural network parameters.
Batch normalization stabilizes training by normalizing layer activations.
Dropout regularization prevents overfitting by randomly zeroing activations.
Learning rate scheduling adjusts the step size during training.
Encoder decoder architecture maps input sequences to output sequences.
Self-attention computes weighted combinations of all positions in a sequence.
Positional encoding injects sequence order information into transformer inputs.
Cross-entropy loss measures the divergence between predicted and true distributions.
Softmax normalizes logits to a probability distribution over the vocabulary.
Spectrogram augmentation SpecAugment applies time and frequency masking for data augmentation.
Conformer architecture combines convolution and self-attention for speech processing.
Residual connections help gradients flow through deep networks.
Layer normalization normalizes activations within each layer.
Vocoder converts acoustic parameters like mel spectrogram to waveforms.
Griffin-Lim algorithm reconstructs waveforms from spectrograms iteratively.
Phone posterior probability measures how likely a phoneme is given acoustic features.
Forced alignment aligns phoneme sequences to audio frames.
Acoustic feature extraction transforms raw waveforms into compact representations.
Reverberation is the persistence of sound after the source has stopped.
Spectral subtraction reduces noise by subtracting an estimated noise spectrum.
DeepFilterNet uses a deep filter network for wideband speech enhancement.
Noise robustness ensures a model performs well in noisy environments.
Room impulse response RIR characterizes acoustic reverberation.
